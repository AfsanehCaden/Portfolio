Heart Failure
Mortality Prediction

Identifying the clinical factors most associated with mortality and building a practical prediction workflow for patients with advanced heart failure.

PythonScikit-learnStreamlitRandom Forest

1 · THE PROBLEM

Can clinical records help identify patients at greater risk?

The project examined 299 patient records collected at two hospitals in Faisalabad, Pakistan. The objective was to determine the attributes most connected with mortality, compare predictive models, and investigate whether important risk patterns differed by gender.

299patient observations
13clinical and lifestyle attributes
3classification models compared
0.896selected model ROC AUC

2 · THE SOLUTION

From exploratory analysis to an interactive prediction application

Explore

Cleaned and examined the clinical dataset, retained medically plausible outliers, analyzed correlations, and compared patterns for women and men.

Model

Built Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest classifiers, with emphasis on recall so fewer at-risk patients would be missed.

Deliver

Selected Random Forest based on the balance of mortality recall and overall discrimination, then developed a Streamlit interface for exploration and prediction.

SELECTED MODEL

Random Forest

The model offered the strongest practical balance for this objective, correctly identifying 80.8% of mortality cases in the reported test results.

85.6%Accuracy
80.8%Recall
72.4%Precision

3 · THE RESULT

A clearer view of risk factors and model tradeoffs

The analysis highlighted follow-up time, serum creatinine, ejection fraction, age, and serum sodium among the strongest correlates of mortality. It also demonstrated why model selection in healthcare should consider the cost of missed positive cases—not accuracy alone.
